# kokoro-malay-bella — build plan

**Goal:** one Kokoro TTS model that speaks Bahasa Melayu in the *Bella* voice, runs in the browser (ONNX, WebGPU with WASM fallback) and in Python, trained on Kaggle's free GPUs with **zero recorded data**.

**Status of this document:** written 2026-11-13. Nothing has been trained or run yet. Facts I checked today are listed in [section 2](#2-what-is-verified-and-what-is-not); everything else is a design decision or an estimate, and each one is covered by a gate that catches it if it is wrong.

---

## 0. The plan in one page

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Base model | `hexgrad/Kokoro-82M` v1.0 (Apache-2.0), fine-tuned, not trained from scratch |
| Training code | `semidark/kikiri-tts` recipe + its patched `semidark/StyleTTS2` submodule. It is the only public Kokoro fine-tune recipe and has been reproduced for German, Polish, Marathi, Arabic, Hebrew, Thai and Kannada |
| Where the Malay audio comes from | **Synthetic.** A Malay-capable zero-shot TTS (Chatterbox Multilingual v3, MIT, `ms` supported, 24 kHz) reads Malay text in Bella's voice, using a Bella reference clip generated by stock Kokoro |
| How much data | **8 hours net** of Malay (about 6,500 clips) + **1 hour** of stock-Kokoro Bella English as an anchor set. Generate about 11 hours raw, expect to discard 20–30% |
| Phonemes | Own rule-based Malay G2P that emits only symbols already in Kokoro's 178-token vocab. Written twice (Python and JS) from one spec, with a parity test. No espeak in the browser |
| Voice | One voicepack, `ms_bella`, 510×256 float32 = 522,240 bytes |
| Deliverables | `model.onnx` (fp32), `model_fp16.onnx`, `model_q8f16.onnx`, `ms_bella.bin`, `g2p` (py + js), Python runtime, web runtime + demo page |
| Shipped size | 86 MB (q8f16, WASM) or 163 MB (fp16, WebGPU) plus 0.5 MB voice. See section 15 for why it cannot be smaller without a second project |
| Compute | About 30–45 Kaggle GPU-hours over 2–3 weeks of free quota. Optional: about US$10–25 of rented GPU does all training in 1–2 days |
| Your time | Two listening sessions of about 15 minutes each (Gate 2 and Gate 8). Everything else is scripted |

### Why this route

1. **Malay needs no new symbols.** Every Malay phoneme already has a token in Kokoro's vocab, and Kokoro has heard most of them in Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Hindi and Japanese. So this is a pronunciation and prosody adaptation, not a new-language build. That is what makes 8 hours enough where the German base model needed 51.
2. **There is no Malay recording of Bella**, and Bella is a synthetic voice. The only way to get Malay audio in her timbre is to generate it. A zero-shot teacher gives native-ish Malay prosody plus Bella's timbre in one step.
3. **A single, consistent synthetic voice is the easy case for a fixed voicepack.** Kokoro's ONNX path has no diffusion sampler; it uses one averaged style vector. That works best when the training voice has low variance, which synthetic data guarantees.
4. **The Indonesian fork (`snowfluke/kukuru-tts`) already measured the trap:** espeak-ng gets e-pepet vs e-taling right for only 5 of 20 common pepet words. That is why this plan uses its own G2P with a lexicon instead of espeak.

### What I would not do

- **Train on espeak `ms`/`id` phonemes.** Mislabelled `e` vowels get baked into the model permanently.
- **Use `mesolitica/Malaysian-F5-TTS-v3` as the teacher for a commercial model.** It is the most Malaysian-sounding option but is CC-BY-NC-4.0. It stays in the plan only as a non-commercial fallback.
- **Use Edge/Azure voices (Yasmin, Osman) as training audio.** Microsoft's terms do not allow training models on the output.
- **Use the `mesolitica/TTS` or `Malaysian-TTS` datasets.** No licence is declared, and they contain cloned voices of real public figures.
- **Use mixed precision.** StyleTTS2 training is unstable in fp16. Everything is fp32.

## 1. Constraints and honest limits

- **"Super small" has a floor.** Kokoro's architecture is 81.76 M parameters. Quantisation takes the file from 326 MB to 86 MB. Getting below that means a narrower network, which means training from scratch, which contradicts "minimal data and compute". Section 15 lays out that follow-on project and when it is worth doing.
- **Quantised models do not run on WebGPU.** The int8 operators fall back to CPU. So the real choice is: WebGPU with fp16 (163 MB) or fp32 (326 MB), or WASM with q8f16 (86 MB).
- **The voice will be the teacher's rendition of Bella**, pulled back toward true Bella by the anchor set and the voicepack choice. Gate 2 and Gate 8 measure how close it is.
- **Accent comes from the teacher.** Whether final `a` is said as [a] or [ə] ("saya" → [sajə]) is inherited from the training audio, not set by the G2P. Gate 2 is where you decide if the accent is acceptable.
- **Kaggle limits** (confirm on your account page before starting): 12 h per session, roughly 30 GPU-hours per week, one P100 or two T4 (16 GB each), about 20 GB persistent output in `/kaggle/working`, internet needs phone verification.

## 5. Phase 1 — Malay front end (1–2 days, no GPU)

This is the most important phase. A wrong G2P cannot be fixed after training without retraining.

### 5.1 Pipeline

`text → normalise → split into sentences → per word: lexicon → rules → phonemes → tokens → chunks of ≤ 510 tokens`

### 5.2 Phoneme inventory (all IDs verified in Kokoro's vocab)

| Sound | Spelling | Symbol | ID | | Sound | Spelling | Symbol | ID |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | a | `a` | 43 | | ny | ny | `ɲ` | 114 |
| e taling | e (lexicon) | `e` | 47 | | ng | ng | `ŋ` | 112 |
| e pepet | e (default) | `ə` | 83 | | c | c | `ʧ` | 133 |
| i | i | `i` | 51 | | j | j | `ʤ` | 82 |
| o | o | `o` | 57 | | sy | sy | `ʃ` | 131 |
| u | u | `u` | 63 | | kh | kh | `x` | 66 |
| p b t d k | same | `p b t d k` | 58 44 62 46 53 | | gh | gh | `ɣ` | 139 |
| g | g | `ɡ` (U+0261) | 92 | | glottal stop | final k, aa | `ʔ` | 148 |
| m n l | same | `m n l` | 55 56 54 | | y | y | `j` | 52 |
| r | r | `r` | 60 | | w | w | `w` | 65 |
| f v s z h | same | `f v s z h` | 48 64 61 68 50 | | stress | — | `ˈ` | 156 |

Punctuation kept: `, . ! ? ; : — …` and space (id 16). Everything else is removed before tokenising.

Design choices, each cheap to A/B in Phase 2 before anything is trained:

- **`r` vs `ɾ`** (id 125). Default `r`.
- **Stress marks on vs off.** Default on: `ˈ` immediately before the vowel of the penultimate syllable; if that vowel is `ə`, move to the final syllable. No stress on about 40 function words (di, ke, yang, dan, ini, itu, …). Kokoro's training data always had stress marks, so keeping them keeps input in-distribution.
- **Diphthongs** `ai au oi` are written `aj aw oj`, and only when word-final (pandai, pulau, amboi). Elsewhere they are two vowels (ba-ik, la-ut, ka-in).

### 5.3 Rules

1. Lowercase, Unicode NFC, strip anything outside letters, digits and kept punctuation.
2. Multi-letter first: `ngg→ŋɡ`, `ng→ŋ`, `ny→ɲ`, `sy→ʃ`, `kh→x`, `gh→ɣ`. Then singles: `c→ʧ`, `j→ʤ`, `y→j`, `g→ɡ`, `q→k`, `x→ks` (word-initial `x→z`).
3. **Final `k` → `ʔ`** (tidak, kakak, budak). Non-final `k` stays `k`. Exceptions live in the lexicon (rakyat, makna, bakti …).
4. **Doubled vowel** gets a glottal stop between: saat → `saʔat`, maaf → `maʔaf`.
5. **The letter `e`:**
   - Strip affixes to find the root: prefixes `me- mem- men- meng- meny- ber- be- ter- te- di- ke- se- pe- pem- pen- peng- per-`, suffixes `-kan -an -i -nya -lah -kah -ku -mu`.
   - `e` inside a prefix is always `ə`.
   - Look the root up in `lexicon_e.json`. Entries are full respellings with `é` for taling: `"kereta": "keréta"`, `"meja": "méja"`, `"besar": "besar"`.
   - Root not found: word-final `e` → `e`; `e` before final `h` or `k` → `e` (boleh, pendek); otherwise `ə`.
6. Vowels `a i o u` map straight through. Final-`a` reduction and i/u lowering in closed final syllables are **not** written by the G2P; the model learns them from the audio.
7. Homographs (perang, semak, sepak): take the more frequent reading. Known limitation, logged in `spec.md`.

### 5.4 Building `lexicon_e.json`

1. Take a Malay frequency list (from the Phase 4 text pool), keep word forms containing `e`, reduce to roots with the stripper above. Expect roughly 3,000–5,000 roots.
2. Label each root from three sources and take the majority: Malay Wiktionary IPA (via WikiPron), `Wikidepia/g2p-id` `schwa_dict.csv` for shared Malay/Indonesian roots, and an LLM pass.
3. Hand-check the 500 most frequent roots. By Zipf's law these cover most running text.
4. Anything where the three sources disagree and it is outside the top 500 goes to a review file; default to `ə`.

### 5.5 Normaliser (`ms_norm`)

| Input | Output |
|---|---|
| `0–9` digits | kosong satu dua tiga empat lima enam tujuh **lapan** sembilan |
| 11–19, tens, hundreds, thousands | sebelas, dua belas, dua puluh, seratus, seribu, juta, bilion |
| `3.5` | tiga perpuluhan lima |
| `ke-3` | ketiga |
| `RM12.50` | dua belas ringgit lima puluh sen |
| `25%` | dua puluh lima peratus |
| `13/11/2026` | tiga belas November dua ribu dua puluh enam |
| `3:30 petang` | pukul tiga tiga puluh petang |
| Phone numbers, IC numbers, OTPs | digit by digit |
| `kg km cm ml °C` | kilogram, kilometer, sentimeter, mililiter, darjah Celsius |
| `Sdn. Bhd.`, `Dr.`, `En.`, `Pn.`, `Hj.`, `dll.`, `yg`, `dgn`, `utk` | sendirian berhad, doktor, encik, puan, haji, dan lain-lain, yang, dengan, untuk |
| Acronyms (`KWSP`, `LHDN`, `SMS`) | spelled with Malaysian letter names (English-style: ke-dabliu-es-pi), from a 26-entry table |

### 5.6 English words

Malaysians code-switch constantly, and a voice agent will meet English words in every call.

- `lexicon_en.json`: about 5,000 English words that actually occur in Malaysian text, pre-phonemised once with misaki's English G2P into Kokoro's **native English** phonemes. The base model already speaks these, and the anchor set (section 8.3) keeps that ability alive.
- At runtime a word is English if it is in `lexicon_en.json` and not a Malay root. Unknown English words fall through to the Malay rules.
- The same JSON file is used by Python and JS, so there is no misaki or espeak dependency at runtime.

### 5.7 Two implementations, one behaviour

Write `ms_g2p.py` first. Generate `tests/golden.jsonl` from it: 2,000 varied sentences with normalised text and phoneme string. Then write `ms_g2p.js` and make it reproduce the golden file exactly.

**Gate 1 — pass when all of these hold:**

- [ ] Every symbol the G2P can emit is in `vocab.json` (assert in a unit test; this catches the ASCII `g` trap).
- [ ] JS output equals Python output on 2,000 of 2,000 golden rows.
- [ ] `e` accuracy ≥ 97% on a 300-word list you or I hand-verified, sampled by frequency.
- [ ] Normaliser passes 150 test vectors covering every row of the table above.
- [ ] No output exceeds 510 tokens after chunking, on a 10,000-sentence stress run.

**If it fails:** fix rules or lexicon and re-run. Do not move on; every later phase consumes this output.

## 6. Phase 2 — zero-training baseline (1 hour, CPU is enough)

Feed Malay phonemes from Phase 1 straight into **stock** Kokoro with `af_bella`. No training.

```python
from kokoro import KModel
import torch, soundfile as sf
model = KModel(repo_id='hexgrad/Kokoro-82M').eval()
pack  = torch.load('af_bella.pt', weights_only=True)      # [510, 1, 256]
ps    = ms_g2p("Selamat pagi, boleh saya bantu encik hari ini?")
audio = model(ps, pack[len(ps) - 1], speed=1.0)
sf.write('baseline.wav', audio.numpy(), 24000)
```

Do this for 40 test sentences under four G2P variants: stress on/off × `r`/`ɾ`.

Why bother:

1. It gives the **baseline number** every later result is compared with.
2. It picks the two open G2P options with data instead of opinion.
3. It produces **Bella reading Malay**, which is one of the two reference-clip candidates for the teacher in Phase 3.
4. If it already sounds usable, you know the fine-tune is polishing, not rescuing.

Scoring: transcribe with `faster-whisper large-v3`, `language="ms"`; compute CER after lowercasing and stripping punctuation.

**Gate 2a — pass when:** 160 clips exist, a CER table for the four variants is saved to `eval/baseline.md`, and the G2P variant is frozen (lowest CER; ties go to stress on + `r`). There is no quality bar here. It is a measurement.

## 7. Phase 3 — teacher bake-off (half a day, about 1 GPU-hour, **needs your ears for 15 min**)

Decide how the Malay Bella audio will be produced before spending quota on thousands of clips.

### 7.1 Reference clips

- **Ref-EN:** 15 s of stock Kokoro `af_bella` reading neutral English.
- **Ref-MS:** 15 s of the Phase 2 baseline (Bella reading Malay phonemes), the cleanest-sounding sentences.

### 7.2 Candidates

| ID | Method | Licence | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Chatterbox Multilingual v3, `language_id="ms"`, Ref-EN, `cfg_weight=0` | MIT | Chatterbox's own advice for a cross-language reference |
| T2 | Same, Ref-MS, `cfg_weight=0.3`, `exaggeration=0.5` | MIT | Reference already in the target language |
| T3 | Real Malay speech (FLEURS `ms_my`, CC-BY-4.0) converted to Bella with Chatterbox VC (`example_vc.py`) | MIT + CC-BY | Truly native prosody; source is 16 kHz so must pass the bandwidth check |
| T4 | `mesolitica/Malaysian-F5-TTS-v3` with a Bella reference | **CC-BY-NC** | Only if the result will stay non-commercial |

Chatterbox pins its own torch version. Run it in its **own notebook**, never in the training notebook.

### 7.3 Test set

40 sentences: 20 general, 10 with numbers/currency/dates, 5 questions, 5 code-switched. Plus a 20-word **e-test** list read in carrier sentences: besar, meja, kereta, emak, enam, empat, teman, merah, boleh, pendek, leher, sewa, beza, perang, elok, esok, ekor, semak, sate, tauge.

### 7.4 Scores

- **CER** as in Phase 2.
- **Speaker similarity:** cosine between ECAPA embeddings (`speechbrain/spkrec-ecapa-voxceleb`) of each clip and a true-Bella centroid. Self-calibrated: `hi` = mean similarity between different stock-Bella clips, `lo` = mean similarity between Bella and `af_sarah`. Normalised score = `(sim − lo) / (hi − lo)`.
- **Bandwidth:** 99% spectral roll-off frequency, averaged per candidate.
- **Your rating**, blind, 10 clips per candidate: "does this sound like a Malaysian speaking naturally?" 1–5. And the e-test: count the words said wrong.

**Gate 2 — a candidate passes when:**

- [ ] mean CER ≤ 4%
- [ ] normalised speaker similarity ≥ 0.6
- [ ] roll-off ≥ 9 kHz
- [ ] your mean rating ≥ 3.5
- [ ] e-test ≥ 17 of 20

**Pick** the passing candidate with the highest rating; break ties on speaker similarity, then licence simplicity (T1/T2 before T3).

**If none passes:**

- CER or naturalness fails everywhere → use T3 for prosody and accept lower speaker similarity (the anchor set and voicepack recover timbre later).
- Only speaker similarity fails → proceed with the best one; this is the weakest requirement because of the anchor set.
- Everything fails → stop and tell me. That is the one point in this plan where the approach itself would need rethinking, and it costs one GPU-hour to find out.

## 8. Phase 4 — text corpus and audio generation (2–3 days elapsed, about 8–10 GPU-hours)

### 8.1 Text pool, about 9,000 prompts → about 11 hours raw

| Share | Type | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 35% | General written Malay | Malay Wikipedia and Tatoeba sentences, 6–20 words |
| 30% | Conversational and call-centre style: greetings, confirmations, appointments, reminders, apologies | LLM-written, reviewed by sampling |
| 15% | Numbers, RM amounts, dates, times, phone numbers, percentages | Templates filled with random values |
| 10% | Code-switched Malay with English words | LLM-written |
| 5% | Questions and exclamations | Mixed |
| 5% | Short replies, 2–5 words ("Ya, baik.", "Terima kasih, puan.") | Hand list, combined so each clip is ≥ 1.2 s |

Selection rules:

- Sentence length 3–22 words, so clips land in 1.5–10 s.
- Greedy selection for coverage: every phoneme ≥ 300 occurrences, every phoneme in word-initial, medial and final position where legal, the 400 most frequent phoneme pairs each ≥ 20 times.
- Remove duplicates and near-duplicates. Remove anything containing a word that Gate 1's e-review file flagged as uncertain.
- Hold out **200 sentences as the test set** (`eval/test_200.txt`) and **100 as validation**. They are never used for training.
- Separately keep 3,000 unused phonemised sentences as `OOD_texts.txt`. The trainer requires this file.

### 8.2 Generation

- Kaggle **T4 ×2**. Two worker processes, one per GPU (`CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0` and `=1`), each taking half the manifest. A two-GPU session costs the same quota as a one-GPU session.
- **Measure first:** generate 50 clips, compute real-time factor, extrapolate the total. If the estimate exceeds 12 GPU-hours, cut the raw target to 9 hours.
- Fixed settings from the Gate 2 winner. One fixed seed per clip, stored in the manifest.
- **Resumable:** skip any clip whose file already exists. Every 500 clips, pack to FLAC and push a shard to the data repo. A session that dies loses at most 500 clips.
- Manifest row: `id, text, norm_text, phonemes, wav, seed, dur, teacher, ref`.

### 8.3 Anchor set, 1 hour, free

Generate 700 English sentences with **stock Kokoro `af_bella`** and its own misaki phonemes. It takes minutes.

What it buys:

- Keeps the decoder's timbre tied to true Bella instead of drifting to the teacher's imitation.
- Stops the model forgetting English, which code-switching needs.
- Gives a set of true-Bella clips to extract a voicepack from in the **new** style space (section 12).

**Gate 3 — pass when:**

- [ ] ≥ 10.5 hours raw Malay generated and uploaded; manifest row count equals file count.
- [ ] 1 hour of anchor audio generated.
- [ ] 30 random clips spot-played: no truncation, no babble.

## 9. Phase 5 — filter and prepare (half a day, about 1.5 GPU-hours)

Per clip, in this order:

1. Resample to 24 kHz mono 16-bit if not already. Trim leading and trailing silence, leave 100 ms padding each side.
2. Loudness-normalise to −23 LUFS. Reject if peak clips.
3. Duration 1.2–12 s, else reject.
4. Speaking rate between 8 and 22 phonemes per second, else reject (catches hallucinated repeats and truncation).
5. ASR round-trip CER ≤ 5% against `norm_text`, else reject.
6. Speaker similarity ≥ 10th percentile of the batch, and normalised score ≥ 0.5, else reject.
7. Roll-off ≥ 9 kHz, else reject.
8. Phoneme length between 10 and 510 tokens (`min_length` in the config is 50 characters; set it to 10 for our short replies).

Then:

- Write `train_list.txt`, `val_list.txt` in the recipe's format `path.wav|phonemes|speaker`. Use the repo's `scripts/prepare_training.py` conventions and confirm by loading one batch.
- Anchor clips go into `train_list.txt` with the same speaker label. Keep a separate file listing which IDs are anchors.
- Recompute phoneme coverage on what survived.

**Gate 4 — pass when:**

- [ ] ≥ 8.0 hours of Malay kept.
- [ ] Rejection rate ≤ 35%. Higher means the teacher is unstable; go back to Gate 2 settings.
- [ ] Coverage targets from 8.1 still met after filtering.
- [ ] A dataloader built from the lists yields one batch with no exception and no token id outside 0–177.
- [ ] 20 random kept clips sound right; 20 random rejected clips are rejected for a visible reason.

**If under 8 hours:** generate more prompts from the pool and repeat Phases 4–5 for the shortfall only.

### 10.5 Before any real run

1. Patch every bare `except:` in `train_first.py` and `train_second.py` to print the exception. A silent failure in the adversarial step otherwise looks like normal training.
2. Confirm `num_workers` is read from the config and not hard-coded.
3. Run 30 steps of Stage 1. Record seconds per step and peak VRAM (`nvidia-smi`).
4. Synthesize one English sentence through the **converted** base weights with stock `af_bella`. It must sound like stock Kokoro. This proves the conversion and the symbol map in one shot.

**GPU choice:** if Kaggle offers the P100, use it for training. It is faster than a single T4 in fp32 and avoids multi-GPU code paths. Otherwise use T4 ×2 but pin training to one GPU. Multi-GPU is an optimisation to try only after a clean single-GPU epoch.

**Gate 5 — pass when:**

- [ ] 178 symbols, all asserts pass.
- [ ] Base weights load with no size mismatch (missing diffusion and discriminator keys are expected).
- [ ] The converted-weights English sample is intelligible.
- [ ] First-step losses are finite and roughly: Mel 0.8–1.5, Gen 3–6, Disc 4–6, Mono 0.01–0.1, S2S 1–6. A NaN Mel almost always means the symbol map is wrong.
- [ ] Peak VRAM ≤ 14 GB at batch 4, `max_len` 400. If not, drop to `max_len` 300, then batch 3.
- [ ] Projected Stage 1 time = steps/epoch × s/step × 6 ≤ 10 hours. If more, reduce to 5 epochs or split across two sessions.

## 11. Phase 7 and 8 — training

### 11.1 Stage 1: acoustics and alignment (estimated 5–9 GPU-hours)

```bash
cd StyleTTS2
accelerate launch --num_processes 1 --mixed_precision no \
  train_first.py --config_path ../configs/config_malay_bella_ft.yml
```

- Run as a Kaggle **Save Version → Save & Run All** so it continues with the browser closed.
- After every epoch a small hook uploads the checkpoint and the TensorBoard event file to the ckpt repo and deletes checkpoints older than the last two from `/kaggle/working`.
- Stage 1 is not optional here: the style encoders are not in Kokoro's public weights and are learned in this stage.

Healthy curve, from the recipe: Mel falls from about 0.8 toward 0.25–0.35; Gen 2.5–3.5; Disc 3.8–4.2; Mono below 0.05; S2S falling.

**Gate 6 — pass when:**

- [ ] Final Mel ≤ 0.35.
- [ ] No NaN, no loss stuck flat from step 1.
- [ ] TensorBoard validation audio is intelligible Malay.
- [ ] `first_stage.pth` exists and is uploaded.

**If Mel plateaus above 0.4:** check five random training pairs by ear against their phoneme strings; then check the symbol map; only then touch the learning rate.

### 11.2 Stage 2: duration, pitch, energy (estimated 10–18 GPU-hours, two sessions)

```bash
accelerate launch --num_processes 1 --mixed_precision no \
  train_second.py --config_path ../configs/config_malay_bella_ft.yml
```

Two-step schedule, because the adversarial phase is where 16 GB cards run out:

- **Epochs 0–3, predictor only.** Batch 4. Cheap and safe. This is where Malay rhythm and intonation are learned.
- **Epochs 4–7, joint adversarial.** Before starting it, run a 30-step probe at batch 2 with the adversarial losses active.
  - Peak VRAM ≤ 14.5 GB and steps keep advancing → run epochs 4–7 at batch 2.
  - OOM, or no step completes in 10 minutes (the reported silent hang) → set `joint_epoch: 999`, run epochs 4–7 predictor-only at batch 4. The model is still complete and usable; you lose some polish, not function.

Session boundary and resume:

- Plan the session split so each ends at an epoch boundary under 11 hours.
- **Resume check, mandatory:** the first logged Mel loss after resuming must be within 15% of the last value before stopping. If it jumps (for example to 2 or 7), the checkpoint did not load. Stop immediately; do not burn quota on a randomly initialised model. Two known causes: `module.` prefix mismatch, and the predictor encoder being re-initialised after load.

Healthy curve: Mel starts near 0.4–0.5 (**not** 7–8) and drifts toward 0.25–0.3; Dur 1.3 → 0.9; CE 0.18 → 0.05; F0 4 → 2; Norm 3 → 1, noisy.

**Per-epoch automatic evaluation.** After each epoch, a script converts the checkpoint to KModel format, extracts a voicepack, synthesizes the 100 validation sentences, and writes one row to `eval/epochs.csv`: CER, normalised speaker similarity, UTMOS (relative signal only), mean speaking rate, voicepack norm.

**Gate 7 — pass when:**

- [ ] Stage 2 started from trained weights (Mel < 1.0 at step 1).
- [ ] At least one epoch has validation CER ≤ 4%.
- [ ] Voicepack norm is stable across epochs (no collapse toward 0, no explosion).
- [ ] The best epoch is identified by CER first, speaker similarity second. It is often not the last one.

**If no epoch reaches CER ≤ 4%:**

1. Listen to the ten worst sentences. If the errors are the same words every time, it is the G2P or the normaliser → fix the front end, and if the fix changes training labels, re-phonemise and rerun Stage 2 only.
2. If errors are spread out → data is short. Generate 6 more hours (Phases 4–5), rerun Stage 1 for 3 epochs from the current checkpoint, then Stage 2.

## 12. Phase 9 — voicepack, selection, acceptance (half a day, **15 min of your ears**)

Take the best two epochs from Gate 7. For each, build these voicepacks:

| ID | Built from | Expectation |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | `extract_voicepack.py` on 200 Malay training clips | best Malay prosody |

**Gate 8 — final acceptance. Ship when:**

- [ ] Test CER ≤ 3%, **and** no worse than the teacher's own CER on the same sentences by more than 1.5 points.
- [ ] Normalised speaker similarity ≥ 0.6.
- [ ] Number/currency/date suite: 50 of 50 read correctly.
- [ ] Short-reply suite (20 two-to-four-word utterances): no clipped first consonant, no trailing noise.
- [ ] Code-switch suite (20 sentences): English words recognisable.
- [ ] Your blind rating of 15 clips ≥ 4.0 of 5, and it beats the Phase 2 baseline in a 10-pair A/B at least 8 times of 10.

## 13. Phase 10 — export and quantise (half a day, CPU only)

### 13.1 To Kokoro format

Convert the chosen checkpoint to the five-component file `KModel` loads: `bert, bert_encoder, predictor, text_encoder, decoder`. Check the parameter count is 81.76 M and that `KModel(config=…, model=…)` loads it with `weights_only=True`.

### 13.2 To ONNX

```bash

```

It builds `KModel(disable_complex=True)` → `KModelForONNX`, opset 17, inputs `input_ids [1,T] int64`, `style [1,256] float32`, `speed [1] float32`; outputs `waveform`, `duration`.

### 13.3 Voice file


### 13.4 Quantised variants

Use `adrianlyjak/kokoro-onnx-export`:

| File | Command flags | Expected size | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `model.onnx` | `export` | ~326 MB | WebGPU fallback, Python GPU |
| `model_fp16.onnx` | `--fp16-threshold=5 --quant-threshold=-1` | ~160 MB | WebGPU default |
| `model_q8f16.onnx` | `--quant-threshold=2 --quant-type QInt8 --quant-exclude '(^/decoder/generator/conv\_post/Conv)'` | ~83–86 MB | WASM, Python CPU |

The `conv_post` exclusion is required. Quantising that layer adds audible static that the tool's own loss metric does not flag.

- [ ] Listen to 5 clips per variant on headphones: no hiss or static in silences.
- [ ] `ms_bella.bin` is exactly 522,240 bytes.

## 14. Phase 11 — runtimes (1–2 days, no GPU)

### 14.1 Python, `python/kokoro_malay.py`

Dependencies: `onnxruntime`, `numpy`, the `g2p/` folder. Nothing else.

```python
ids   = [0] + [vocab[c] for c in ms_g2p(ms_norm(text)) if c in vocab] + [0]
style = voice[min(len(ids) - 2, 509)][None, :]                 # voice: (510, 256)
wav   = sess.run(None, {'input_ids': np.array([ids], np.int64),
                        'style': style.astype(np.float32),
                        'speed': np.array([1.0], np.float32)})[0]
```

Also ship the PyTorch path (`KModel` + `ms_bella.pt`) for anyone who wants it.

### 14.2 Web, `web/kokoro-malay.js`

Plain `onnxruntime-web`. Not kokoro-js: it bundles the espeak phonemiser and a hard-coded voice list, neither of which we want.

- Runs in a Web Worker.
- Backend choice: if `navigator.gpu` exists and the adapter has `shader-f16` → WebGPU + `model_fp16.onnx`. If WebGPU without f16 → WebGPU + `model.onnx`. Otherwise → WASM + `model_q8f16.onnx`.
- Self-test on first load: synthesize a fixed short phrase and check the output is finite and not near-silent. If the fp16 path fails the self-test, fall back to the next option automatically.
- Wrap tokens in `0 … 0`. Without this the first consonant is eaten.
- Speed is the model's `speed` input, never `playbackRate`.
- Long text: split at sentence punctuation, synthesize chunk by chunk, play as each finishes.
- Cache the model with the Cache Storage API so the download happens once.
- Multi-threaded WASM needs `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin` and `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy: require-corp` on the hosting page.

**Gate 10 — pass when:**

- [ ] JS G2P matches Python on the golden file in the browser, not only in Node.
- [ ] Web output vs Python ONNX output for the same text: `duration` identical, mel L1 < 0.15.
- [ ] Works on: Chrome and Edge desktop (WebGPU), Chrome Android (WebGPU or WASM), Safari (WASM at minimum).
- [ ] Real-time factor recorded per device. Target: < 0.3 on desktop WebGPU, < 1.0 on laptop CPU with WASM.
- [ ] Second page load performs no model download.
- [ ] Python package: `pip install onnxruntime numpy` in a clean venv, run the example, get a correct WAV.

## 15. "Super small": what you get and what it would take to go further

### What this plan ships

| Target | File | Download |
|---|---|---|
| Browser, any device, WASM | `model_q8f16.onnx` + voice | ~86 MB |
| Browser, WebGPU | `model_fp16.onnx` + voice | ~163 MB |
| Python CPU | `model_q8f16.onnx` | ~86 MB |
| Front end | G2P + lexicons | well under 1 MB |

One voice instead of 54 already saves 28 MB of voice files. There is nothing else to remove from an 82 M-parameter network without retraining.

### Follow-on project (not part of this plan)

A half-width Kokoro student: `hidden_dim` 256, decoder `upsample_initial_channel` 256, `max_conv_dim` 256. Roughly 20–25 M parameters, about 25 MB quantised or 45 MB fp16.

- **Data is free:** the finished Phase 1 model generates 50 hours of consistent Bella Malay in about one GPU-hour.
- **Compute is not:** it is training from scratch. Expect a few hundred T4-hours, which is months of Kaggle quota or roughly US$50–100 of rented GPU.
- **Risk is real:** no public recipe exists for a narrowed Kokoro. Treat it as research.

**Do it only if** the 86 MB WASM build is measured to be a product blocker (for example, load time on Malaysian mobile data). Decide after Gate 10 with real numbers.

## 18. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Effect | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teacher's Malay sounds foreign or Indonesian | Medium | Model inherits it | Gate 2 catches it for one GPU-hour; T3 uses real Malaysian speech instead |
| Teacher misreads é/ə while our labels are right | Medium | Noisy labels for some words | e-test in Gate 2; ASR filter; T3 fallback |
| Stage 2 adversarial phase does not fit 16 GB | Medium–high | Slightly less polish | Predictor-only fallback is defined and still ships |
| Resume silently loads nothing | Medium | Wasted session | Mandatory 15% loss check |
| 8 hours is not enough | Low–medium | CER above target | Scale-up rule in Gate 7 |
| fp16 on WebGPU produces noise | Medium | Larger download on WebGPU | Automatic fallback to fp32 or WASM |
| Kaggle session killed mid-epoch | Certain, eventually | Lose under one epoch | Upload every epoch, resumable generation |
| Recipe or fork changes under us | Low | Breakage | Pin commit hashes in Phase 0 |
| Chatterbox dependency pins break Kaggle's environment | Medium | Lost setup time | Separate notebook, never the training one |
